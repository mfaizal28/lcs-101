What Careers Can You Get with Computer Science?

Computer Science is one of the most versatile qualifications you can have. Whether you go to university or start working after sixth form, a CS background opens doors across dozens of industries. Here are the main career paths and what they actually involve.

Software development

The most direct career path. Software developers build the apps, websites and systems that power modern life.

  • What you'd do: Write code, fix bugs, design systems, work in teams using agile methodologies
  • Languages: Python, JavaScript, Java, C#, Swift, Go — depends on the company and product
  • Salary range: £25k–£45k starting, £60k–£100k+ with experience
  • Path: CS degree or coding bootcamp, then junior developer role

Sub-specialisms include frontend (what users see), backend (servers and databases), mobile (iOS/Android), and full-stack (both).

Cybersecurity

One of the fastest-growing fields. Cybersecurity professionals protect organisations from hackers, data breaches and digital threats.

  • What you'd do: Penetration testing, security auditing, incident response, threat analysis
  • Salary range: £28k–£40k starting, £55k–£90k+ senior
  • Path: CS degree + security certifications (CompTIA Security+, CISSP), or apprenticeships

The UK has a significant shortage of cybersecurity professionals, making it one of the most in-demand career paths in tech.

Data science and AI

Data scientists analyse large datasets to find patterns, make predictions and inform business decisions. AI/ML engineers build systems that learn from data.

  • What you'd do: Statistical analysis, machine learning models, data visualisation, natural language processing
  • Salary range: £30k–£45k starting, £60k–£120k+ senior
  • Path: CS or Maths degree, often a Master's for research roles

Game development

If you love gaming, game development lets you build them. It's competitive but rewarding.

  • What you'd do: Game programming (physics, AI, graphics), level design, engine development
  • Languages: C++, C#, Lua, plus game engines (Unity, Unreal)
  • Salary range: £22k–£35k starting, £45k–£80k senior
  • Path: CS degree or game development degree, portfolio of personal projects

Web development

Every business needs a website. Web developers build and maintain them.

  • What you'd do: Design and build websites, e-commerce platforms, web applications
  • Languages: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, plus frameworks (React, Vue, Angular)
  • Salary range: £24k–£35k starting, £45k–£75k senior
  • Path: CS degree, bootcamp, or self-taught with a strong portfolio

Network engineering

Network engineers design, build and maintain the infrastructure that connects computers — from office networks to global internet systems.

  • What you'd do: Configure routers and switches, monitor network performance, troubleshoot connectivity, implement security
  • Salary range: £25k–£35k starting, £45k–£70k senior
  • Path: CS degree or apprenticeship + Cisco certifications (CCNA, CCNP)

IT consulting

IT consultants advise businesses on how to use technology effectively. It combines technical knowledge with business skills.

  • What you'd do: Assess client needs, recommend solutions, manage technology projects
  • Salary range: £28k–£40k starting, £55k–£100k+ senior/partner
  • Path: CS degree + business experience, often at a consulting firm (Accenture, Deloitte, etc.)

Beyond traditional tech roles

Computer Science skills are increasingly valuable outside of pure tech:

  • Finance — algorithmic trading, fintech, quantitative analysis
  • Healthcare — health informatics, medical imaging, NHS Digital
  • Education — EdTech, curriculum development, teaching
  • Creative industries — digital art, animation, music technology
  • Research — bioinformatics, computational physics, climate modelling

Do you need a degree?

For many tech roles, the answer is increasingly "not necessarily." Companies like Google, Apple and IBM have dropped degree requirements for some positions. What matters is demonstrable skills — which you can build through:

  • Degree apprenticeships (earn while you learn)
  • Coding bootcamps (intensive 12-16 week programs)
  • Self-teaching with projects and certifications
  • Open-source contributions and portfolios

That said, a CS degree remains the most reliable path for competitive roles and gives you the theoretical foundations that self-teaching often misses.

Whatever route you take, building practical skills early matters. Start with hands-on Python challenges and test your theory with auto-marked quizzes — both are free and designed for GCSE and A-Level students.
